# The AI Energy Crisis No One's Talking About

**Date:** February 2026  
**Category:** ai, energy, infrastructure

---

## The Core Problem

Modern AI data centres demand power equivalent to mid-sized cities. The numbers:

- **Google search:** 0.3 Wh per query
- **ChatGPT request:** 2.9 Wh per query (~10x more)
- **AI energy demand:** 8 TWh (2024) → **652 TWh by 2030** (80x increase in 6 years)

Power grids were never designed for this much demand appearing this quickly in concentrated locations. Transmission lines and substations take years to build. AI infrastructure advances in months.

---

## The Jet Engine Solution

Companies aren't waiting for grids to catch up. They're taking power generation into their own hands:

- **Crusoe Energy** (OpenAI partner): Ordered 29 gas turbines from Boom Supersonic
- **xAI**: Using methane-fuelled turbines (and getting fined for it)
- **Decommissioned aircraft engines** (like PE6000): Being converted to mobile power plants

Park them near a data centre, hook up fuel, get off-grid power in days.

Industrial turbines are sold out through 2029.

---

## The Contradiction

The most advanced computing systems ever built are now relying on aviation hardware and burning fossil fuels to stay online.

These turbines emit:
- Nitrogen oxides (carcinogenic, causes asthma)
- Carbon dioxide

The "temporary" solutions risk becoming permanent infrastructure.

---

## The Long-Term Bet: SMRs

Amazon and Google are looking at **Small Modular Nuclear Reactors**:
- Continuous, low-carbon power
- No intermittency (unlike wind/solar)
- Faster construction, lower costs than traditional reactors

Jet engines are the symptom. The real bottleneck isn't chips or data — it's electricity.

---

## India Opportunity

The MRO (Maintenance, Repair, Overhaul) sector could capture value here:
- Refurbish decommissioned jet engines for stationary power
- India is pushing both data centre investment and domestic MRO capacity
- High-precision engine specialists are uniquely positioned

---

## Key Insight

> The future of artificial intelligence will be shaped as much by energy policy as by algorithms.

Until grids adapt — through upgrades, faster transmission build-outs, and scalable clean power — companies will keep reaching for whatever works, even if it looks outdated.
