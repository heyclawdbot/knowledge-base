# Quantum Is the New AI: Arvind Krishna on What Enterprises Are Missing

**Source:** [Smart Talks with IBM - Malcolm Gladwell interviews Arvind Krishna (IBM CEO)](https://youtu.be/8X7VYhHKbss)  
**Date Captured:** 2025-02-14

---

## TL;DR

IBM CEO Arvind Krishna argues that **quantum computing today is where GPUs and AI were in 2015**. Enterprises that start learning quantum now will have a 5-year head start when the "Netscape moment" arrives. The playbook? Stay narrow, pick domain-specific problems, and don't try to boil the ocean.

---

## Key Insight: Quantum Today = AI in 2015

> "Quantum today is where GPUs and AI were in 2015. And I bet you every AI person is thinking and hoping 'I wish I had started doing more in 2015' as opposed to waited till 2022. Quantum today is there. So it's not good enough that you can get a big advantage, but if you learn how to use it, then in 5 years you'll be ready to exploit what comes."

The message is clear: **Don't wait for the "Netscape moment."** By the time quantum computing becomes obvious, the learning curve advantage will be gone. Early movers who invest in understanding the technology now will dominate later.

---

## The Enterprise AI Playbook (That Most Are Missing)

Krishna outlines IBM's differentiated approach to AI for enterprises — advice that applies equally to quantum:

### 1. Don't Go Wide, Go Deep

> "If you're focusing on the enterprise that actually takes away the focus of having to go to extremely large models which by definition are going to be computationally expensive, power hungry, and demand lots and lots of data."

**Consumer AI** needs to do everything — write haikus, speak Finnish, compose emails in Steinbeck's voice — because each feature adds millions of users.

**Enterprise AI** doesn't need any of that. You need a model that does **one thing extremely well** for your specific domain.

> "If my goal is to help a company summarize the legal documents in English, that can be a model that's a hundredth the size — as effective, probably higher quality — but I don't need to go wide."

**Key takeaway:** Enterprise AI isn't about the biggest model. It's about the most focused one.

### 2. Stay in Your Domain

> "We are very focused on helping those problems that can give people immediate benefit where we have domain knowledge. Our domain knowledge is around operations, programming and coding, customer service, customer experience, logistics, procurement. Let's stay in the areas where we have a lot of expertise."

Don't chase every AI use case. Pick the ones where you already have:
- Deep expertise
- Quality training data
- Clear success metrics
- Measurable ROI

### 3. Eat Your Own Cooking First

> "We kind of apply it to ourselves and so we are not asking our clients to be the first experiment on it. We say you can leverage what we did. We're happy to bring out all our learnings including what needs to change in the process."

Before selling AI transformation, do it internally. Then bring the playbook — including the failures — to clients.

### 4. The Biggest Change Isn't Technology

> "The biggest change is not technology. It's getting people to accept that there's a different way of working."

Process change > tech deployment. Most AI projects fail not because the model doesn't work, but because people don't change how they work.

## Why Quantum Matters: A New Kind of Math

Krishna explains quantum computing not as "faster computers" but as **an entirely different kind of computation**:

> "A quantum computer operates in the equation domain. It doesn't need to explode the state space. It can actually solve it. That's why I called it a different kind of math."

### Problems Quantum Loves:
- **Materials science:** Simulating how materials behave (years → seconds)
- **Optimization:** Traveling salesman problems, logistics routing
- **Cryptography:** Breaking and building new encryption

### Example: Post Office Optimization

> "A post office in a mid-size country probably burns a billion gallons of fuel per year delivering packages. Today our heuristics get us to within 20% of the optimal answer. Let's suppose a quantum computer can get you the next 10%. If I can get 10% of a billion gallons — that's 100 million gallons. In the country I'm thinking about, that could be 800 million pounds of saving to one entity in one year."

---

## The Timeline: 3-5 Years to "Shocking People"

> "We have probably four to five years from that moment. That's why it's under-discussed — because the moment I say 'new kind of math' I probably lost 99% of the audience."

The "Netscape moment" for quantum is 3-5 years away. But unlike AI where you could catch up post-2022, quantum has a **steeper learning curve**. Starting now is an investment in future readiness.

---

## What Enterprises Should Do Today

Based on Krishna's framework:

1. **Identify quantum-compatible problems** in your domain (optimization, simulation, materials)
2. **Start small experiments** — IBM has ~200 clients doing exactly this
3. **Build internal expertise** — learn the concepts even if you can't use them yet
4. **Watch the curve** — 3-4 years from now, production use cases will emerge
5. **Don't wait for perfect** — the learning advantage compounds

---

## Meta-Lesson: How IBM Bets on Emerging Tech

Krishna's approach to quantum reveals his broader decision-making framework:

1. **Ask engineering questions first:** "Can we make a computer, not just a science experiment? Can it run by itself all night?"
2. **Find constraint points:** "If I could find more [quantum researchers], I would hire them. I'm constrained on people."
3. **Calibrate pressure:** "You got to be careful. If you push too hard on timing, you'll get people to take so much risk that actually the thing will fail."
4. **Allow pushback:** "The team knows they're allowed to push back and really argue back hard. That means you'll get to probably that correct Goldilocks pressure."

---

## Bottom Line

Quantum computing is **5 years away from mainstream enterprise adoption**. But the learning window is now. The same AI regret of "I wish I started in 2015" will repeat for quantum.

The playbook:
- **Narrow focus** → domain-specific problems
- **Small models** → tuned for your use case  
- **Learn early** → compound the knowledge advantage
- **Process first** → technology follows

Don't chase the frontier. Pick your lane and own it.
